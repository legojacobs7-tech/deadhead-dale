# What works for Deadhead Dale — the learning log

Bill asked (2026-11-25) for the numbers to be checked every day so the scripts and videos keep getting better.
The daily stats run updates this file every morning. The script-ideas run and the nightly production run read it
before they write or make anything.

How to use it:
- **Scoreboard** — one row per episode per platform, refreshed daily (numbers keep growing for ~3–7 days).
- **Lessons** — what the data says, each with evidence and a confidence level. Upgrade, downgrade or delete lessons
  as data comes in; never keep a lesson the numbers no longer support.
- **Rules in force** — the short list the ideas/production runs must actually apply. Bill's own instructions in
  CLAUDE.md always win over anything learned here; when data disagrees with Bill, tell Bill instead of overriding him.
- **Experiments** — one or two deliberate tests at a time, so we learn something instead of guessing.

Metric notes: *shares* are the strongest growth signal, then *saves*/*comments*, then *avg watch time* and
*retention* (% of the video watched), then likes. "Watched past 3 s" (IG reel view rate) measures the hook.
Compare episodes at the same age (e.g. 48 h after posting), not raw totals — older posts have had more time.

## Lessons (as of 2026-11-25 — only 3 data points, all low confidence)

1. **Geese + an everyday errand gets shared.** One Screw (geese take over after a hardware-store run) got 15 shares;
   every other episode got 1. It's also the premise Bill loves most. *Confidence: low (one standout).*
2. **Length isn't what's hurting.** People watch about the same share of every video (37–42%) whether it's 39 s or 61 s,
   so the 61-s One Screw actually got the most watch time. Don't cut scripts short just to be short; cut lines that are weak.
   *Confidence: low-medium.*
3. **Everyone leaves at about the same point, so the middle is where we lose people.** A ~70% 3-second hold means the
   opening works; most viewers leave around the 40% mark. A strong surprise around the middle may keep them.
   *Confidence: low (needs retention curves, which we don't have).*
4. **Open question: does "very random" do better or worse than "story with a twist"?** The two story episodes (One Screw,
   Unexpected Goose) beat Haircut so far, but Haircut is only 12 h old. Bill asked for much more random scripts on 11-24,
   so we keep doing that. Watch Haircut, Microwave, Tuesday and Gas Station Sushi at 48 h against One Screw and
   Unexpected Goose. If the random ones keep losing, tell Bill and suggest a blend (random swerves inside a simple
   errand). Don't change the style without him.

## Rules in force (apply these when writing ideas / making videos)

- Bill's rules in CLAUDE.md come first (random, natural swearing, loops, 15–25 lines).
- In every daily batch, include at least one idea built on an everyday errand or chore that goes wrong, preferably with
  geese or another running gag (lesson 1).
- Write one "shareable" line per script: a moment people would send to a specific friend. Make the caption's
  "Send this to someone who..." line point at that moment.
- Put the weirdest swerve around 40–50% of the way through, where viewers start leaving (lesson 3).
- Don't trim a good script just to make it shorter (lesson 2).
