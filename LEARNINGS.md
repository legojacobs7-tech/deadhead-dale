# What works for Deadhead Dale — the learning log

Bill asked (2026-11-25) for the numbers to be checked every day so the scripts and videos keep getting better.
The daily stats run updates this file every morning. The script-ideas run and the nightly production run read it
before they write or make anything.

How to use it:
- **Scoreboard** — one row per episode per platform, refreshed daily (numbers keep growing for ~3–7 days).
- **Lessons** — what the data says, each with evidence and a confidence level. Upgrade, downgrade or delete lessons
  as data comes in; never keep a lesson the numbers no longer support.
- **Rules in force** — the short list the ideas/production runs must actually apply. Bill's own instructions in
  CLAUDE.md always win over anything learned here; when data disagrees with Bill, tell Bill instead of overriding him.
- **Experiments** — one or two deliberate tests at a time, so we learn something instead of guessing.

Metric notes: *shares* are the strongest growth signal, then *saves*/*comments*, then *avg watch time* and
*retention* (% of the video watched), then likes. "Watched past 3 s" (IG reel view rate) measures the hook.
Compare episodes at the same age (e.g. 48 h after posting), not raw totals — older posts have had more time.

## Lessons (re-checked 2026-11-27 with fresh Instagram numbers — 4 reels at 1.5–4 days, 1 at 21 h)

1. **Shares come from a premise everybody has lived, and a caption that names that person.** One Screw ("Tag someone who's been to
   the hardware store 4 times this week") 15 shares; The Microwave ("Tag someone whose kitchen appliances are definitely in a cult")
   7 shares at only 38 h; everything else 0–2. Geese alone don't do it (Unexpected Goose: 1 share). *Confidence: low-medium (2 of 5 reels, same pattern).*
2. **Instagram views level off around 1,300–1,650 per reel after ~2 days, whatever the style.** One Screw went 1,629→1,639 and Goose
   1,445→1,457 in two days; Haircut and Microwave are already at ~1,360. With 4 followers, Instagram tests each reel on a similar-size crowd
   of strangers and stops, so views don't tell the episodes apart — shares and follows are what can break the ceiling. *Confidence: medium.*
3. **Length isn't what's hurting.** People watch 37–42% of every video, 38 s or 61 s, so longer videos get more watch time.
   Don't cut scripts short just to be short; cut weak lines. *Confidence: medium (5 reels now).*
4. **Most viewers leave around the 40% mark.** 3-second hold is 67–73% (the openings work), avg watch is ~40% of the video.
   A big swerve near the middle is our best shot at holding them. *Confidence: low (no retention curves from Metricool).*
5. **"Very random" is not losing to "story with a twist".** At ~2 days the random reels (Haircut 1,366 views / 2 shares, Microwave 1,356 / 7)
   sit level with the stories (Goose 1,457 / 1, One Screw 1,639 / 15). The story reels hold a slightly better 3-s hook (69–73% vs 67%).
   So the data backs Bill's "much more random" rule; what matters more is lesson 1 (a relatable target). *Confidence: low-medium.*
6. **Facebook watches far less.** Thanksgiving on FB: 181 views, 6 s avg watch (12%) vs 19 s (39%) on IG. *Confidence: low (one reel, 21 h).*
7. *Watch:* Thanksgiving had 484 IG views at 21 h vs Haircut's ~1,200 at 22 h — maybe the holiday, maybe the audience now being split across
   4 platforms. No action until Parade Balloon and the Black Friday episodes show whether it repeats.

## Rules in force (apply these when writing ideas / making videos)

- Bill's rules in CLAUDE.md come first (very random, swear in most lines placed naturally, loops, 15–25 lines, 35–50 s+).
- Every daily batch: at least two ideas whose starting point is something nearly everyone has done (hardware-store run, self-checkout,
  a kitchen appliance, a haircut, returns line) — then go as random as Bill wants from there (lessons 1, 5).
- Caption share line = "Tag/Send this to someone who..." + a specific, common, slightly embarrassing habit (the One Screw / Microwave
  pattern), not a generic "someone who'd love this".
- Write one "shareable" moment per script — the line people would send to a specific friend — and point the caption at it.
- Put the weirdest swerve around 40–50% of the way through (lesson 4).
- Don't trim a good script just to make it shorter (lesson 3).
